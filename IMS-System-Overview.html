Inventory & Operations Management System

The Arboreal Labs IMS

One system that runs a manufacturing or distribution business end to end — from a customer order, through the bill of materials, purchasing, goods receipt and quality inspection, into QR-tracked stock, job work, finished goods and dispatch. Every stock number is derived from an unchangeable ledger, and every document is signed off before it can leave the building.

It is built by Arboreal Labs, which makes custom operations software for manufacturers and distributors. The first deployment runs the operation of a water-treatment equipment manufacturer — but nothing in it is specific to water treatment. The same system fits any business that buys parts, holds stock, builds or resells, and needs to know where its material and money went. This document walks through every screen, in plain language.

This is a demo view. Every screen is a faithful reproduction of the application's real layout — page titles, columns, status labels and the navigation are taken straight from the software — filled with invented sample data. It is not any real company's records.

1What the IMS is

Most small manufacturers run on a stack of spreadsheets, a WhatsApp group and a few people who "just know" where things are. That works until it doesn't — a job ships short, a payment goes out twice, stock is counted but can't be found, and nobody can say which customer a batch of material was consumed on.

The IMS replaces that with one connected record of the business. It covers:

  • Master data — your products, the parts inside them, your suppliers, reusable bills of materials and inspection checklists.
  • Orders & planning — a customer order becomes a project; the project explodes into a bill of materials; the BOM is compared against stock to find the shortfall.
  • Procurement — purchase orders with per-line price approval and a digital signature chain; nothing prints until it is approved and signed.
  • Receiving & quality — goods receipt against the PO, an optional inspection gate, then automatic creation of QR-labelled stock lots.
  • Stock — every lot is a QR code; on-hand quantity is recomputed from an immutable movement ledger, never edited by hand.
  • Job work — send raw material to an outside vendor and receive the finished part back, cost = raw + process rate.
  • Output — serialised finished units, dispatch, and a scan-the-QR traceability history.
  • Oversight — a live "action center" of discrepancies, supplier scorecards, per-project costing, and an activity log that even catches direct database edits.

Two ideas hold it together and run through every screen: one ledger (stock quantity is only ever the sum of signed movement rows) and one audit trail (every record change is logged with who and when). Section 6 covers both.

2Who it is for

The system is modular. A screen you don't need is simply not used — there is no penalty for leaving job work, inspection, or the BOM engine switched off.

Assemble-to-order equipment manufacturers

You sell configurable products — a machine, a plant, a panel — where each order has options, and the parts list changes with those options. The first deployment is exactly this — a water-treatment manufacturer where every system is a model plus a set of choices, and the bill of materials follows the choices.

Central: Master data, BOM templates, Projects, Requisitions, POs, GRN, Inventory, Finished Goods.   Optional: Job Work, Inspection.

Fabrication shops & job shops

Your work is cutting, machining, bending, plating — often with steps sent out to specialists. Raw stock is issued out against a signed job-work order, the finished part comes back through goods receipt, and its cost carries both the raw material and the process rate.

Central: Components, Vendors, Job Work, GRN, Inventory, Traceability.

Distributors & trading businesses

You buy, store and resell — no production. Run purchase orders into goods receipt into QR-tracked stock, issue against customer orders, and use supplier KPIs and the reconciliation checks to keep the operation honest.

Central: Vendors, POs, GRN, Inventory, Traceability, Supplier KPIs, Action Center.   Switched off: BOM templates, Job Work, Finished Goods.

Multi-project / contract manufacturers

You run several customer jobs at once and need to keep their material and cost separate. Every requisition, PO line, goods receipt and stock movement can be tagged to a project; stock can be blocked so it is reserved to one job; per-project costing shows budgeted vs ordered vs received vs consumed at any moment.

Central: Projects, project-tagged Requisitions/POs, stock blocking, Project Reports, per-project costing.

Quality-regulated production

Incoming material must pass inspection before it can be used, and you need the paperwork to prove it. Attach a checklist to a component and its goods receipt becomes a gated flow: no stock lot exists until a manager approves the inspection with their signature.

Central: Inspection Templates, the inspection gate at GRN, the auto-generated inspection report, Traceability.

3Built for one client, fitted to the next

The first client is a water-treatment equipment manufacturer, and the system was shaped around how they work — their product families, their annotated bill-of-materials sheet, their incoming-inspection form and its document number, their fiscal-year numbering (JW/2026-27/0001). None of that is hard-wired into the product. Those are examples of the system being fitted to one business's process — and the same fitting is done for the next.

Change by configuration

  • Roles, and which roles see pricing and cost
  • Who signs each document type, and in what order
  • Inspection checklists — per component, per field
  • How each part is tracked: one QR per piece, per box, or measured bulk
  • How a part is counted: numbers, length, or weight
  • Which supplier supplies which part, at what price and lead time
  • BOM templates and the rules that pick parts from an order's options
  • Which parts are job-work, their raw supplier, process vendor and rate
  • Purchase-order terms, GST rate and delivery text

Change by adapting the build

  • Printed document layouts — the PO template, the inspection report
  • The names and gates of the project phases
  • Document numbering schemes and prefixes
  • Whether a given step must reference a PO, or may stand alone
  • The rules behind each reconciliation / discrepancy check
  • Entirely new screens for a step unique to your business

You do not adopt someone else's process.

The system is fitted to how you already work. Where your process is genuinely different, the configuration bends to it; where it needs code, the code is written for your process rather than the other way around. There is no "standard workflow" you are forced to follow.

5The screens, one by one

Each feature is described in plain terms — what it does and why it matters — beside a reproduction of the actual screen with sample data.

5.1  Signing in & who sees what

Four roles, and a pricing curtain

Everyone signs in with an email and password — there is no public sign-up; accounts are created by an admin. Each person is Admin, Founder, Team Lead or Team Member. Roles decide what you can change and whether you can see money. A Team Member works the shop floor — receives goods, scans and consumes stock — but every price and cost column is hidden from them.

Why it matters Cost data is commercially sensitive. The curtain is drawn in the database view layer, not just hidden in the screen, so it holds even if someone pokes at the raw data.

5.2  Master Data

The hub, and the parts master

Master Data is the reference everything else is built from: products and their options, components (the parts), categories, BOM templates, inspection templates, vendors and which parts they supply, customers, and approval rights (who signs what). Each component carries the attributes that make the rest of the system behave: its tracking mode (QR per piece / per box / bulk), whether it is a job-work part, and whether it needs inspection before becoming stock.

Why it matters Set a part up once and goods receipt, inspection, costing and QR labelling all do the right thing for it automatically.

BOM templates — one template, every configuration

A template line is common (always included), variant-driven (the part and quantity are chosen from a rule based on the order's options), or an assembly line that pulls in a sub-assembly's own template. When a project BOM is generated, the engine resolves every variant line against the options the customer actually chose.

Why it matters A ten-option product has hundreds of possible parts lists. You maintain one template with rules; the system produces the exact list for each order.

5.3  Projects / Orders

The project list & phase stepper

A customer order becomes a project with a number, a customer, the customer's PO number and value, and a phase: planning → document approval → procurement → production → dispatched → closed (with an on-hold override). The stepper on each row shows exactly where the job is.

Why it matters Anyone can open the list and see which jobs are moving and which are stuck.

The project workspace

The project detail page is the operational heart of a job. A phase banner nudges the next action; below it, collapsible panels cover line items, the bill of materials (Not generated → Draft → Approved), job work, stock status & blocking, materials issued, shortfall, and site purchase. Finance roles also see a costing card.

Why it matters Everything about a job — what it needs, what's on order, what's short, what's been consumed — is on one page, measured against the approved BOM.

Project reports

Three Excel exports for procurement follow-up and accounts reconciliation: the approved BOM (locked until it is approved), a material status sheet classifying every part as Not Ordered / PO Raised / Received / Consumed, and an accounts view with one row per component stacking every PO, date, vendor, quantity, rate and invoice number against it.

Why it matters Your buyer and your accountant get the exact view they need, in the tool they already use, without asking anyone to compile it.

5.4  Requisitions

Indents, and issuing stock against them

A requisition is a tracked demand — an indent — usually tagged to a project, moving through open → partially ordered/issued → ordered/issued → closed. From an open requisition you can issue matching stock, or use receive & consume: scan a lot's QR with the device camera (or type the code), set the quantity, and it is consumed. Consuming stock with no project is admin-only and demands a written reason.

Why it matters Demand is captured and closed out on the record, and consumption happens at the point of use with a scan — not reconstructed later from memory.

5.5  Purchase Orders

From draft to sent — two gates

A PO is created as a draft, then two gates stand between it and "sent". First, every line's price must be approved — by the configured PO approver, with a signature. Second, the signature chain must be complete: creator, then each configured approver. Editing a PO that has already been sent forks a revision; the original is preserved and marked superseded.

Why it matters No PO leaves the building at a price nobody approved, or without the sign-offs your business requires. The rule is enforced right at the print screen.

5.6  Job Work

Send raw, receive finished, carry the cost

A job-work order sends specific raw stock lots out to a process vendor. Signing the order dispatches it — the raw lots are issued out of stock. The finished parts come back through goods receipt; the completed lot's cost is the raw material plus the job-work rate, and it is linked back to the raw lot it came from.

Why it matters Outsourced steps stay on the books — you always know what is out at a vendor, what it cost to process, and which raw batch a finished part traces to.

5.7  Goods Receipt

Receiving against the PO

A goods receipt records a challan number and/or an invoice number — at least one is required, and each must be unique for that vendor. Lines are received one at a time, and every line must reference an open PO line — receiving with no PO is refused. When the line is saved, a database trigger creates the stock lot(s), records the receipt movement, and rolls the quantity up against the PO.

Why it matters What you received is always tied to what you ordered, so PO fulfilment and three-way matching are automatic rather than a monthly chore.

5.8  Inspection (the quality gate)

No stock until it passes

If a component has an inspection checklist attached, its goods receipt does not create stock directly. An inspection record is raised with the checklist answers, and the real stock line — and therefore the QR lot — is created only when a manager approves the inspection, with their signature. Rejected inspections can be re-submitted. Every inspection, approved or rejected, is kept in a filterable register.

Why it matters Material that hasn't passed QC literally cannot be issued — as far as stock is concerned it does not exist yet — and PO fulfilment never counts goods still sitting in inspection.

5.9  Inventory

On-hand, from the ledger

The inventory list shows each component's quantity on hand, lot count, and (for finance roles) value. The number is never entered — it is the sum of that lot's signed movement rows. A component bought and then fully consumed still appears, at quantity zero, so its history stays visible. One click builds an Excel export with a per-PO price breakdown — vendor, rate, GST, PO number, GST/PAN — per component.

Why it matters Stock figures can't drift, because there is no figure to drift — only movements that add up.

5.10  Traceability

Scan any QR, see its whole life

Point the camera at a component's QR (or type its lot code) and get the full history — which PO and supplier it came from, any job work it went through, its inspection record, and every consumption against a project.

Why it matters When a customer or an auditor asks "where did this material come from", the answer is one scan away.

5.11  Finished Goods

Serialised units, in production to dispatched

Each completed unit gets a serial number and its own QR, is linked to the project line item it fulfils, and moves through in production → ready → dispatched. Shop staff see the status; managers change it.

Why it matters Warranty, service and recall all start from a serial number that ties back to the exact build and its parts.

5.12  Action Center

Six live checks against the ledger

One screen surfaces discrepancies as they arise: BOM vs ordered vs received variance, receipts with no PO, BOM demand never ordered, stock untouched for over 60 days, overdue PO lines, and invoice value vs PO value. Each is a database view — always current — and each section shows a green "all clear" when empty.

Why it matters The problems that usually surface at stock-take or audit surface here the day they happen, with a link straight to the fix.

5.13  Supplier KPIs

A scorecard per vendor

Total, open and completed POs; percentage of lines fulfilled; percentage delivered on time; average lead time in days; and the vendor rating — all built from the same PO and goods-receipt data the rest of the system runs on.

Why it matters Supplier reviews backed by your own transaction history, not impressions.

5.14  Users & activity log

Admin-provisioned accounts, one activity log for everything

There is no public sign-up. An admin creates each account with a name, email, temporary password and role. Deactivating an account — not deleting it — removes access while keeping history intact. The Log tab is a unified feed across record changes, stock movements, signatures and notifications; the record-change half is written by a database trigger, so it captures a direct database edit just as it captures an in-app one.

Why it matters "Who changed this, and when" always has an answer, and it can't be sidestepped by going around the app.

5.15  My Signature

One signature, used everywhere you sign off

Each person sets up a signature once — typed in a chosen font, or drawn — and picks a default. It is then used to sign purchase orders, goods receipts and job-work orders, and to approve prices and inspections. Every approval dialog checks you have one and prompts you to create it if not.

Why it matters A document's signing state always knows who has signed, who is next, and — if it is stuck — exactly whose signature it is waiting on.

6The backbone: one ledger, one audit trail

Everything above rests on two mechanisms — worth understanding on their own because they are what make the numbers trustworthy.

The stock-movement ledger

Stock quantity is never stored as a number you can edit. Every receipt, issue, adjustment, transfer and return is a signed row — positive or negative — in one append-only table. A lot's quantity on hand is the sum of its rows, recomputed after every change. Consuming stock adds a negative row; correcting a mistake adds a compensating row. The history is never rewritten. The lot page below shows the ledger for one QR-tagged lot.

The audit log

A generic trigger on around fifteen core tables writes an insert / update / delete diff to one audit table — the old values, the new values, who, and when. Because it lives in the database, it records a change made with a SQL client exactly as it records one made in the app. That feed is what the Log tab under Admin → Users shows. Combined with QR lot codes tying physical stock to the record, this is what lets any quantity or any edit be traced back to its cause.

7Security & access control

Access rules are enforced in the database, not just the interface, so they hold no matter how the data is reached:

  • Row-level security on every table. A signed-in user only sees the rows their role and project access allow.
  • Sensitive writes go through vetted procedures. Inspections, signatures, price approvals and the audit log have no direct write access at all — every change runs through a security-checked database function.
  • Column-masked views for non-finance staff. A Team Member's queries return "safe" views with the cost columns removed — visible in the screens above, where the Team Member's Inventory and Components lists carry no value column.
  • No public sign-up. Accounts are provisioned by an admin; access is removed by deactivation, which preserves history.

8Technology

The system is a web application. There is nothing to install — it runs in any modern browser on a phone, tablet or desktop, and the QR scanning uses the device camera. It is built on Next.js 15 and React 19, with Supabase (PostgreSQL) for the database, authentication and row-level security. Excel export and QR generation are built in; there are no third-party services in the data path.

The database is defined as a numbered sequence of migration files — roughly eighty of them — so the entire schema and every business rule in it is version-controlled and reviewable, and a new environment is built by replaying them in order.

BAppendix B — Glossary

BOM — Bill of Materials
The list of parts and quantities that go into a product or a job. A template BOM is reusable per product; a project BOM is the expanded, agreed list for one order.
GRN — Goods Receipt Note
The record of a delivery arriving, entered against the purchase order, line by line. Printed as "MRIN" in the first client's deployment.
IRN — the inspection record
An incoming-inspection checklist result. Where a component requires one, no stock is created until the IRN is approved with a signature.
Lot / QR lot
A tracked unit of stock — a piece, a box, or a measured batch — identified by a QR code. Its quantity is the sum of its ledger movements.
Ledger (stock movements)
The append-only table of every signed quantity change. The single source of truth for on-hand stock.
Requisition
A tracked internal demand (an indent), usually tagged to a project, closed out by ordering or issuing against it.
Job work
An outsourced process step: raw stock is sent to a vendor and the finished part received back, its cost = raw + process rate.
Untagged receipt
Goods received without a project tag. Flagged on a worklist so they can be assigned later.
Tracking mode
How a component's stock is counted and QR-labelled: item (one QR per piece), box (one QR per container), or bulk (one measured lot).
Blocked / issued stock
A lot reserved exclusively to one project. Another project cannot consume it.
Shortfall
What a project's approved BOM still needs after accounting for stock on hand, open orders, and material sent to job work.
Site purchase
A small local buy that is purchased and consumed in one step, logged for costing without entering the store.
Revision
A new version of a PO or job-work order created when the original has already been sent. The original is preserved and marked superseded.
Signature chain
The ordered set of signers a document needs — creator first, then configured approvers — before it can be sent or printed.
